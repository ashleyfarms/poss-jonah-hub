Secretariat: The Coin Toss, the Records and the Giant Heart

Strange History · November 17, 2026 · By Poss Jonah

Some sports records fall within a few years. Secretariat set two in 1973 that people are still chasing more than half a century later. His story starts before he was even born, with a coin toss.

The coin toss

Two owners, Meadow Stud and Ogden Phipps, had an unusual deal over foals sired by the famous stallion Bold Ruler. In the fall of 1969 they flipped a coin. The winner got first pick of the foals born in 1969; the loser got the remaining 1969 foal plus first pick of the foals due in 1970. Because one of the mares did not get in foal, only one foal was due in 1970, so the loser would actually end up with two foals. Penny Chenery of Meadow Stud later said both sides hoped to lose. Phipps won the toss. Meadow Stud, the loser, got the 1970 foal, and that foal was Secretariat, born March 30, 1970, in Virginia. He was a big chestnut colt, and the farm's staff nicknamed him "Big Red," the same nickname once used for Man o' War.

The team behind him

Secretariat was trained by Lucien Laurin and ridden in all three Triple Crown races by Canadian jockey Ron Turcotte. Meadow Stud was run by Penny Chenery, who had taken over her ailing father's farm and its racing stable. The same team had won the 1972 Kentucky Derby and Belmont with another Meadow horse, Riva Ridge. Secretariat's two-year-old season in 1972 was so strong that he was named Horse of the Year, which almost never happens for a two-year-old. Early in 1973, before he had run a single Triple Crown race, he was syndicated for breeding for a then-record $6.08 million, which helped Meadow Stud pay estate taxes after Chenery's father died.

The Kentucky Derby

On May 5, 1973, Secretariat won the Kentucky Derby in 1:59 2/5, which is still the track record for the race at Churchill Downs. What amazes racing fans is how he did it: each quarter mile was faster than the one before. His quarter-mile splits were about :25 1/5, :24, :23 4/5, :23 2/5 and :23. Most horses slow down as a long race goes on. Secretariat sped up.

The Preakness

Two weeks later he won the Preakness Stakes at Pimlico. The official time was disputed for decades because of a timer malfunction. In 2012, after a review of the race film, the Maryland Racing Commission recognized his time as 1:53, a stakes record.

The Belmont: 31 lengths

The Belmont Stakes, on June 9, 1973, is the race people remember most. It is a mile and a half, the longest of the three Triple Crown races. Secretariat won by 31 lengths in 2:24. That time is still the American record for a mile and a half on dirt. TV viewers watched the camera pull back farther and farther just to keep the second-place horse in the picture. He became the first Triple Crown winner in 25 years, the first since Citation in 1948.

The giant heart

Secretariat was retired to stud at Claiborne Farm near Paris, Kentucky, at the end of 1973. He died there on October 4, 1989, at age 19. The veterinarian who performed the necropsy reported that his heart was about twice the normal size for a horse. The heart was not weighed, so numbers you sometimes see, like 22 pounds, are estimates. Many people believe that huge heart helps explain how he could keep accelerating.

Why he still matters

Secretariat was named Horse of the Year in both 1972 and 1973 and is in the National Museum of Racing Hall of Fame. He appeared on the covers of national magazines in 1973, which almost never happens for a racehorse. Visitors still go to Claiborne Farm to see where he rests. His records are a reminder that sometimes the legend is not an exaggeration. Read about another famous horse rivalry: Seabiscuit vs. War Admiral.
