Look inside the kindergarten workbook

What's inside: 76 pages for numbers 1–20

  • Number pages: a big numeral and number word to see and say, rows to trace and write, and simple apples, stars, acorns, fish and hearts to count and color.
  • Practice pages: color the boxes and circle the group to build number sense.
  • Review pages: count and circle, match the number, which group has more or fewer, ten frames, number words, fill in the missing number, and counting by ones to 20.
  • Two dot-to-dots from 1 to 20.
  • Extras: a “Great job!” certificate, a note for grown-ups, and a “This book belongs to” page.

It's printed in black and white on 8.5 × 11 inch pages, so kids can color everything. Amazon lists the reading age as 3–6 years.

How homeschool families can use it

A gentle daily rhythm. Do one number page on day one and its practice page on day two. Every few days, do a review page. That's about ten minutes a day, and it fits before or after a read-aloud. Spread out this way, the book lasts most of a semester.

Use it with real objects. Before the page, count real things: buttons, beans, spoons at the table. Then do the same number on paper. Moving from hands-on to paper is how numbers start to stick.

Use it with any program. The workbook doesn't follow a particular curriculum. It's extra practice you can use next to whatever math program you already use, or on its own for a preschooler who is ready.

Questions families ask

What age is it for?

Amazon lists ages 3–6. It suits preschoolers who are ready to write numbers and kindergartners working up to 20.

Is it in color?

No, it's black and white on purpose, so children can color the pictures.

Does it include answers?

Most pages are counting and tracing with the answer in plain sight. Review pages are short enough for a parent to check at a glance.

Can I copy pages for my kids?

The book is meant for one child to write in. For more children, buy a copy for each child, or have them work on a sheet protector with a dry-erase marker.

Is it Christian?

It's a plain math workbook with no Bible lessons. It's made by Poss Jonah, who also writes the Christian books on this site, and it fits faith-based homeschools.
