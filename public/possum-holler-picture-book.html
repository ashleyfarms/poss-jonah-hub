Look inside the picture book

Three stories, three lessons

Syl and the Sour Grapes: a story about pride

Syl the fox wants the grapes hanging just out of reach. When she can't get them, she decides they must be sour anyway. Children see how easy it is to put something down just because we can't have it.

The Long Road Home: some folks wander, some stay

Two bunny twins leave the Holler for the bright lights of the big city. Things don't go the way they planned, and they find their way back home. It's a gentle story about home, family, and the folks who welcome you back.

Bernard Badger and the Muddy Road: judge a critter by his help

Grumpy Bernard Badger has a KEEP OUT sign on his burrow. But when a stranger gets stuck on a muddy road, Bernard is the one who stops to help. Kids learn that kindness shows in what we do, not how we look.

The book ends with “Poss's Three Morals,” a page that sums up the lesson of each story in a sentence.

Talking about the stories as a Christian family

The stories are told as Holler tall tales, not Bible stories. Many families like to pair each one with a Bible passage for a short talk after reading:

  • Sour grapes and pride: Proverbs 16:18 and Philippians 4:11–12, on being content.
  • The Long Road Home: the parable of the lost son in Luke 15:11–32, and a Father who welcomes us home.
  • Bernard Badger: the Good Samaritan in Luke 10:25–37. Who was a neighbor to the stranger?

Questions to ask: Why did Syl say the grapes were sour? When have you felt like that? What made the twins want to go home? Who surprised you by being kind?

For homeschool read-alouds

Each story is short enough for one sitting. Use the book for narration (have your child retell the story), story sequencing (beginning, middle, end), and character education.
