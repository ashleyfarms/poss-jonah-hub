Well, That's Weird: History

The Windsor Pumpkin Boat Regatta

True, odd moments from history, told short and checked against at least two sources.

The story

Every fall in Nova Scotia, folks have raced across a lake in hollowed-out giant pumpkins. Meet the Windsor Pumpkin Regatta on Lake Pesaquid in Windsor, Nova Scotia — pumpkin capital country. Danny Dill started it in nineteen ninety-nine. His dad, Howard Dill, bred the Atlantic Giant pumpkin. That first year, five brave souls hollowed out giant pumpkins and paddled them across the lake.

The course is about half a mile. Crowds grew into the thousands, and towns from Wisconsin to Vermont copied the idea. Danny Dill said you need three things: water, a giant pumpkin, and somebody crazy enough to paddle it. What really happened? A weigh-off town turned Halloween gourds into boats. Pure, delightful weird.

The Pumpkin King

You cannot tell the story of the regatta without Howard Dill (1934 to 2008), a farmer from Windsor, Nova Scotia, known as "The Pumpkin King." Dill left school in grade six and taught himself plant breeding. In 1979 one of his hybrids won the top prize at the International Pumpkin Association weigh-off at 439 pounds, and that same year he patented the seed variety he called Atlantic Giant. In 1980 he set a world record with a 459-pound pumpkin. Atlantic Giant seeds went on to be grown by competitive growers around the world, and the giant pumpkins you see at fairs today descend largely from his work.

How a pumpkin becomes a boat

Giant pumpkins that weigh hundreds of pounds are mostly water and have a thick wall around a big hollow center. Cut a hole in the top, scoop out the seeds and pulp, and the shell floats. A grown person can kneel or sit inside and paddle. In Windsor, the boats were brightly painted and decorated, and racers wore costumes. The race covered a half mile (800 meters) on Lake Pesaquid, and there were three classes: paddling, motor and experimental. The paddling class was the most popular by far.

Danny Dill, Howard's son, started the regatta in 1999. By 2008 it drew about 10,000 spectators and 60 entries. One racer, Leo Swinimer of New Ross, Nova Scotia, won six of the first ten races, including one at age 73. In 2005, Martha Stewart announced she would race and even had a pumpkin prepared, but her trip fell through, and her pastel pumpkin went in without her. Local member of Parliament Scott Brison raced several times.

Who was first, and who copied it

Windsor was not the very first place someone paddled a pumpkin. That honor goes to Wayne Hackney of Winchester, New Hampshire, who paddled a pumpkin he grew in 1996. But Windsor turned it into a festival, and others followed, including a regatta at the University of Wisconsin-Madison held since 2005 and one on Lake Champlain in Colchester, Vermont.

Where things stand

The last Windsor regatta was held in 2019. The 2020 and 2021 events were canceled during the COVID-19 pandemic. Then in 2022 the government ordered the tidal gates under the Highway 101 causeway to be operated in a way that helps fish pass through. That lowered the water in Lake Pesaquid, and the event was canceled indefinitely. Other towns still hold their own pumpkin races, so the idea Danny Dill made famous lives on, even if its home lake is lower than it used to be.

Quick facts

  • Where: Lake Pesaquid, Windsor, Nova Scotia, Canada
  • Founded: 1999 by Danny Dill
  • Course: half a mile (800 meters)
  • Classes: paddling, motor and experimental
  • Peak crowd reported: about 10,000 spectators and 60 entries in 2008
  • Last held in Windsor: 2019
  • The pumpkin: Atlantic Giant, bred and patented by Howard Dill in 1979
