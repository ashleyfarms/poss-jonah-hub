The "Bear" That Wrecked a Rolls-Royce Was a Costume

Odd, non-graphic true-crime stories and courtroom curiosities.

The story

Lake Arrowhead, California, January 2024. A claim says a bear climbed into a Rolls-Royce and clawed up the seats. There's even video. Then two more claims come in, for two Mercedes, same day, same place, same kind of bear. Officials put the total at $141,839. The insurers got suspicious and called the state. Investigators showed the videos to a wildlife biologist.

The biologist took one look. "Clearly a human in a bear suit. Detectives say they found a furry bear costume, with metal claws, at a home. Four people were charged with insurance fraud. In April 2026, three pleaded no contest and got weekend jail time. The fourth case is still open.

The claims

The California Department of Insurance called its investigation "Operation Bear Claw." According to the department, an insurance company flagged a claim about an incident on January 28, 2024, in Lake Arrowhead, a mountain resort town in San Bernardino County where black bears really do live. The claim said a bear had gotten into a 2010 Rolls-Royce Ghost and damaged the interior. Video was submitted to prove it.

Detectives then found two more claims, filed with two different insurance companies, with the same date and the same location. One was for a 2015 Mercedes G63 AMG and the other for a 2022 Mercedes E350, and both again included video of a "bear" inside the car. The total paid out or claimed across the three companies, according to the department, was $141,839.

How the costume was caught

Bear break-ins are real in California mountain towns, which is probably why the scheme seemed believable. But when investigators looked closely, the footage did not look right. To be sure, the Department of Insurance asked a biologist from the California Department of Fish and Wildlife to review all three videos. The biologist's opinion was that it was clearly a human in a bear suit. Detectives then served a search warrant and found the bear costume at one of the suspects' homes. The Glendale Police Department and the California Highway Patrol assisted, and the San Bernardino County District Attorney's Office handled the prosecution.

Arrests and outcome

In November 2024 the department announced that four Los Angeles-area residents had been arrested on insurance fraud and conspiracy charges, and that a warrant had been issued for a fifth person. In April 2026 the department announced that three of the defendants had pleaded no contest to felony insurance fraud. Each was sentenced to 180 days in jail, to be served through a weekend jail program, plus two years of supervised probation, with restitution ordered. The case against a fourth defendant was still pending, with a court date set for later in 2026. Because that case is unresolved, that person should be presumed innocent.

Why it matters

It is easy to laugh at a costume caper, and the department itself called the scheme bizarre. But insurance fraud is not a victimless prank. Insurers pass the cost of fraud on to every policyholder through higher premiums, which is one reason states have whole fraud divisions to chase stories like this one.

If a real bear ever does get into your car, the California Department of Fish and Wildlife's standing advice is simple: do not leave food, coolers or anything scented in a vehicle in bear country, keep windows up and doors locked, and report bear conflicts to the department. And if your claim comes with a video, make sure the bear in it is a bear.

Quick facts

  • Investigation name: Operation Bear Claw (California Department of Insurance)
  • Date and place of the claimed attacks: January 28, 2024, Lake Arrowhead, California
  • Vehicles: a 2010 Rolls-Royce Ghost, a 2015 Mercedes G63 AMG and a 2022 Mercedes E350
  • Amount: $141,839 across three insurers
  • Key evidence: a state wildlife biologist's review of the videos, and the costume found in a search
  • Outcome so far: three no-contest pleas and sentences announced April 2026; one case pending
