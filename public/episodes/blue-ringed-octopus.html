Blue-Ringed Octopus

Real animals that sound made up. Every claim in these episodes is checked against published science or museum sources.

The story

Meet the blue-ringed octopus. Shy, tan, and about as big as a golf ball. It hides in tide pools and reefs from Japan to Australia, hunting crabs. But bother it, and in under a second, its rings flash electric blue. That's the warning. Tiny muscles pull back skin to uncover layers that reflect blue light. The warning is real. Its venom is tetrodotoxin, the same poison found in pufferfish. It blocks nerve signals, and the bite is so small you might not feel it. There's no antivenom, so doctors keep the patient breathing until it wears off.

Where does it get the poison? Scientists think bacteria in its spit glands may help make it. But it saves the venom for crabs. Bites are rare, almost always from someone picking one up. So look, don't touch. And if anyone's ever bitten, call emergency services right away.
