Look inside: real pages from the poem and the nativity

What's inside this Christian Christmas book for children

The book has three parts, and each one does a different job on Christmas Eve.

1. A Holler family Christmas Eve (pages 1–9)

The story opens in Possum Holler on Christmas Eve. Jeri Lynn gathers the young critters around the fireplace and opens a book. It's a gentle way in for little listeners, who see kids like them settling down to hear a story before bed.

2. Moore's poem, as he wrote it (pages 10–37)

“A Visit from St. Nicholas,” better known as “The Night Before Christmas,” was first published in 1823 and is credited to Clement Clarke Moore. All fourteen stanzas appear in Moore's own words, each one set in large type across from a full-page painting. The Holler folks act out the poem: the stockings, the clatter on the lawn, the eight tiny reindeer, and Santa Zeb with his round belly and snow-white beard.

3. The nativity story from the Bible (pages 38–71)

Then Poss Jonah takes over. Sitting by the fire with the Holler kids, he tells them it's “the best story ever told.” Each of the next 34 pages pairs a realistic painting of the Bible scene with a speech bubble from one of the Holler critters. The bubbles retell the story in plain words for kids ten and under. Short Scripture quotes come from the World English Bible, and each page gives its reference, like Luke 2:7. Many pages end with a one-line Why it matters for kids to remember, such as “Jesus came for everybody, rich or poor.”

The nativity part follows the Gospels in order, beginning with the promises of a coming King in Isaiah and Micah. Next come Zechariah and Elizabeth, Gabriel's visit to Mary, Joseph's dream, the trip to Bethlehem, the manger, the shepherds and the angels, Simeon and Anna in the Temple, the wise men, and the family's escape to Egypt. On the last page, the whole Holler gathers around the true meaning of Christmas.

Why families choose a nativity picture book that includes the classic poem

Many Christian parents love the cozy tradition of “The Night Before Christmas” but don't want Santa to be the whole story. This book doesn't make you choose. Kids get the poem they'll hear everywhere in December, and on the very next page they learn why the day exists. The two parts are kept clearly separate. The poem is presented as a fun poem, and the nativity is presented as the true story from the Bible.

For homeschool families

  • Poetry memorization: Moore's poem has strong rhythm and rhyme. Let children learn the opening stanza and recite it on Christmas Eve.
  • Bible memory: Pick one Why it matters line or one quoted verse a week to memorize.
  • Narration: After a reading, ask your child to tell the scene back in their own words, then draw it.
  • Compare and contrast: Older kids can talk through how a fun poem and a true account are different kinds of writing.

For Sunday school and children's church

The big paintings read well to a small group. The Scripture reference on each page makes it easy to open a Bible next to the book. The nativity pages work on their own as a short lesson on any Sunday in Advent.
