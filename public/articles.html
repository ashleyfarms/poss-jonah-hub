Articles: The World of the Strange

Fact-checked stories about odd but true things from history, science, crime and the animal world. They are non-graphic and family-friendly, and every one lists its sources at the bottom. When sources disagree, we say so.

Many of these articles go with a Poss Jonah video. For the short videos and their full write-ups, including cryptid legends and real animals that sound made up, see the Videos page.

  • Why Franz Ferdinand Was Targeted: The Road to Sarajevo, 1914 (Strange History): Why did a 19-year-old pick the heir to Austria-Hungary as his target? A plain-language case file on the causes behind the shot that started World War I.
  • Seabiscuit vs. War Admiral: The Family Feud of 1938 (Strange History): The undersized underdog and the Triple Crown champion were both descended from Man o' War. Here is the true story of their famous 1938 match race.
  • Secretariat: The Coin Toss, the Records and the Giant Heart (Strange History): Secretariat's owners got him by losing a coin toss before he was born. Fifty years later, two of his 1973 records still stand.
  • Marilyn Monroe, Drone Builder and Troop Entertainer: Two Wartime Stories (Strange History): Before she was famous, Norma Jeane built target drones for the Army. Ten years later she left her honeymoon to perform for troops in Korea.
  • Nicholas Winton and the 669 Children of 1939 (Strange History): A London stockbroker skipped a ski trip, went to Prague, and helped organize trains that carried 669 mostly Jewish children to safety in Britain.
  • The Pitch Drop Experiment: The World's Slowest Drip (Strange Science): A professor poured tar-like pitch into a funnel in 1927. Only nine drops have fallen since, and nobody has ever seen one fall.
  • Hiroo Onoda: The Soldier Whose War Lasted Until 1974 (Strange History): A Japanese officer stayed at his post in the Philippine jungle for 29 years after World War II ended, until his former commander came to order him out.
  • The Sailing Stones of Death Valley: How Rocks Move on Their Own (Strange Science): For decades nobody saw the rocks of Racetrack Playa move. In 2013 two cousins finally caught it happening, and the answer was thin ice.
  • The Strangest Olympic Marathon: St. Louis, 1904 (Strange History): A runner rode in a car, another stopped for a nap, and the winner got a dose of poison. The 1904 Olympic marathon was chaos.
  • The Day the Mona Lisa Walked Out of the Louvre (Strange Crime): In 1911 a museum worker took the Mona Lisa off the wall and left. Nobody noticed for more than a day, and the theft made her world famous.
