About Poss Jonah

Poss Jonah is a possum who leans on a lamppost and hunts for the good stuff, town to town. He runs this site and a family of "best of" rankings, and tells true stories that sound too strange to be real.

What you'll find here

Best-of rankings. Best Tea started it, with Southern iced tea by city, and the family now includes pizza, BBQ, fried chicken, seafood, burgers, steak, hot dogs, Chinese, Mexican, fast food, burritos, tacos, and egg rolls. Visitors nominate and cheer for their favorites. The rankings are user-driven and positive only: we celebrate the good places and don't run "worst of" lists.

World of the Strange. We also write short, fact-checked stories about odd but true things from crime, history, and science. We keep them non-graphic and family-friendly, we say plainly when sources disagree, and we list our sources at the bottom of each one. Read them on our Articles page.

Our video series

Most of what we write here starts life as a short video on the Poss Jonah YouTube and TikTok channels. Each series has its own rules:

  • Well, That's Weird: History. True, odd moments from history, checked against at least two sources, like the candy once sold as chicken feed or the pumpkin boat races of Nova Scotia.
  • Well, That's Weird: Animals. Real animals that sound made up, such as the glass frog that hides its blood and the flying squirrel that glows pink. Claims are checked against published science or museum sources.
  • Well, That's Weird: Cryptids. Creatures people report but science has never confirmed. We tell where the legend comes from, what people say they saw, and the sensible explanations, and then let you decide. We never present a legend as proven.
  • Well, That's Weird: Crime. Odd, non-graphic true-crime stories and courtroom curiosities, using court records and official press releases.
  • World War One: A Deep Dive. A long-form history series written so readers and viewers from about age 11 and up can follow it, with disputed points named and explained.
  • Horse racing history. Short profiles of famous racehorses such as Seabiscuit, War Admiral and Secretariat, with every number checked against Hall of Fame records.

How we check facts

Every article lists its sources at the bottom. We prefer primary documents, museum and university pages, court records, government releases and established reference works. When sources disagree on a date or a number, we say so instead of picking the most dramatic version. If a fact cannot be verified, it stays out. Folklore is labeled as folklore, and jokes in the videos stay in the videos; the written articles stick to what the record shows.
